#m1 #linearAlgebra 

Given a linear map $f:E\to F$,

The dimension of the map is:
$$
	\text{dim}(E) = \text{dim}(\text{Ker}(f)) + \text{dim}(\text{Im}(f))
$$
Kernel of a map:
$$
	\text{Ker}(f) = \{u\in E: f(u) = 0_{F}\}
$$
Image of a map:
$$
	\mathrm{Im}(f) = \{ u\in F : f(v) = u \}
$$
#### Example:
$f·\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^3$
$$
	f(\begin{pmatrix}
	x \\
	y \\
	z
	\end{pmatrix}) = \begin{pmatrix}
	 x+z \\
	 y+z \\
	 0
	\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
	B = \{ \begin{pmatrix}
	1 \\
	0 \\
	0
	\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}
	0 \\
	1 \\
	0
	\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}
	0 \\
	0 \\
	1
	\end{pmatrix} \}
$$
$$
	\text{Im}(f) = <f(\begin{pmatrix}
	1 \\
	0 \\
	0
	\end{pmatrix}),f(\begin{pmatrix}
	0 \\
	1 \\
	0 \\
	\end{pmatrix}),
	f(\begin{pmatrix}
	0 \\
	0 \\
	1
	\end{pmatrix})> = <\begin{pmatrix}
	1 \\
	0 \\
	0
	\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}
	0 \\
	1 \\
	0 \\
	\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}
	1 \\
	1 \\
	0
	\end{pmatrix}> = <\begin{pmatrix}
	1 \\
	0 \\
	0 \\
	\end{pmatrix},
	\begin{pmatrix}
	0 \\
	1 \\
	0
	\end{pmatrix}>
$$

Assume $f:E\to F,\text{ }M(f)$, then
- $\text{f is injective} \iff rank(M) = \text{dim }E$
- $\text{f is surjective} \iff rank(M) = \text{dim } F$
- $\text{f is bijective} \iff rank(M) = \text{dim } E = \text{dim } F$

So:
- $\text{dim } E \neq \text{dim } F \implies$ not bijective
- $\text{dim } E > \text{dim } F \implies$ not injective
- $\text{dim } E < \text{dim } F \implies$ not surjective

#### 1)
$f:\mathbb{R^3} \to \mathbb{R^2} \text{ } f\begin{pmatrix}x \\  y \\  z \\  \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}x+2y+z \\  2x-z\end{pmatrix}$
We know dim $E$ = 3 and dim $F$ = 2 $\implies$ not injective.
$$
	f\begin{pmatrix}
	1 \\
	0 \\
	0 \\
	\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
	1 \\
	2
	\end{pmatrix}
	\hspace{5em}
		f\begin{pmatrix}
	0 \\
	1 \\
	0
	\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
	2 \\
	0 \\
	\end{pmatrix}
	\hspace{5em}
	f\begin{pmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
1
\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
-1
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
	M(f) = \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 2 & 1 \\
	2 & 0 & -1
	\end{pmatrix} \sim \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 0 & -\frac{1}{2} \\
	0 & 1 & -1
	\end{pmatrix}
	\implies \text{ rank}(M) = 2 \implies \text{f is surjective}
$$

#### 2)
$f : \mathbb{R^3} \to \mathbb{R^3} \hspace{1em} f\begin{pmatrix}x \\  y \\  z\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}x+y+z \\  -x+2y \\  y-3z\end{pmatrix}$
$$
	M(f) = \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 1 & 1 \\
	1 & 2 & 0 \\
	0 & 1 & -3
	\end{pmatrix} \sim \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 2 & 0 \\
	0 & 1 & -3 \\
	1 & 1 & 1
	\end{pmatrix} \sim \begin{pmatrix}
	-1 & 2 & 0 \\
	0  & 1 & -3 \\
	0 & 3 & 1
	\end{pmatrix} \sim \begin{pmatrix}
	-1 & 2 & 0 \\
	0 & 1 & -3 \\
	0 & 0 & 10
	\end{pmatrix} \implies rank(M(f)) = 3 
$$
$\implies$ f is **bijective**

#### 3 )
$f: \mathcal{P_{2}}(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathcal{M_{2}}(\mathbb{R}) \hspace{2em} f(a+bx+cx^2) = \begin{pmatrix}a & a+b \\  a-b & 2b-c\end{pmatrix}$
$dim(\mathcal{P_{2}}(\mathbb{R})) = 3 < dim(\mathcal{M_{2}}(\mathbb{R})) = 4$

Canonical base of $\mathcal{P_{2}}(\mathbb{R}) = \{ 1,x,x^2 \}$
Canonical base of $\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) = \{ \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0 \\0 & 0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0 & 1 \\  0 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 \\  1 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0 \\  0 & 1\end{pmatrix} \}$
$$
	f(1) = f(1\cdot 1 + 0x + 0x^2) = \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 1 \\
	1 & 0
	\end{pmatrix}
	\hspace{2em}
	f(x) = f(0\cdot 1 + 1x + 0x^2) = \begin{pmatrix}
	0 & 1 \\
	-1 & 2
	\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
	f(x^2) = f(0 \cdot 1 + 0x + 1x^2) = \begin{pmatrix}
	0 & 0 \\
	0 & -1
	\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
	M(f) = \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 0 & 0 \\
	1 & 1 & 0 \\
	1 & -1 & 0 \\
	0 & 2 & -1
	\end{pmatrix}
$$
#### 7.18)
> Let E and F be vector spaces over $\mathbb{R}$ with bases $B_{E} = \{ b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}\}$, $B_{F} = \{ v_{1},v_{2} \}$,
> $f(xb_{1}+yb_{2}+zb_{3}) = (x-2z)v_{1}+(y+z)v_{2}$. Find $M_{B_{F}}^{B_{E}}(f)$

$B_{E}' = \{ b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3},2b_{1}+2b_{3},3b_{3} \}$, $B_{F}' = \{ 2v_{1},2v_{2} \}$

$f(b_{1})= f(1\cdot b_{1} + 0b_{2}+0b_{3})=v_{1} \hspace{2em} f(b_{2}) = f(0b_{1}+1b_{2}+0b_{3}) = v_{2} \hspace{2em} f(b_{3}) = f(0b_{1}+0b_{2}+1b_{3}) =-2v_{1}+v_{2}$
We know dim $E = 3$ and dim $F = 2$ $\implies$  $M_{B_{F}}^{B_{E}}(f)$ will have dim $F$ many columns and dim $E$ many rows. $\implies$  $M_{B_{F}}^{B_{E}}(f) \in \mathcal{M}_{2 \text{x} 3}(\mathbb{R})$ 
$$
	M_{B_{F}}^{B_{E}}(f) = \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 0 & -2 \\
	0 & 1 & 1
	\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
M_{B_{F'}}^{B_{E}}(f) = P_{B_{F'}}^{B_{F}}M_{B_{F}}^{B_{E}}(f) = \begin{pmatrix}
\frac{1}{2} & 0 \\
0 & \frac{1}{2}
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 & -2 \\
0 & 1 & 1
\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
\frac{1}{2} & 0 & -1 \\
0 & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
	P_{B_{E}}^{B_{E'}} = ((b_{1})_{B_{E'}},(b_{2})_{B_{E'}},(b_{3})_{B_{E'}}) = \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 2 & 0 \\
	1 & 0 & 0  \\
	1 & 2 & 3
	\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
	M_{B_{F}}^{B_{E}} = P_{B_{F'}}^{B_{F}}M_{B_{F}}^{B_{E}}(f)P_{B_{E}}^{B_{E'}} = \begin{pmatrix}
	\frac{1}{2} & 0 & -1 \\
	0 & \frac{1}{2} & \frac{1}{2}
	\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 2 & 0 \\
	1 & 0 & 0 \\
	1
	\end{pmatrix}
$$
